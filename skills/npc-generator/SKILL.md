# NPC Generator

A memorable NPC is a person, not a stat block. Give them one thing the players can see, one thing they want, and one thing they are hiding — and the players will do the rest.

## Before you build — ask for what matters

Ask only for what is missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Role** — shopkeeper, quest-giver, villain, ally, bystander [ask, or pick from context]
2. **Setting / tone** [the campaign's setting, or a generic fantasy town]
3. **How important** — throwaway, recurring, or central [recurring]
4. **Anything the DM already knows** — name, job, a fact the party already learned [none — invent it, and say so]

One NPC per call, unless the user asks for a set. For a set, give 3 and keep each one shorter.

## The rules

- **Quirk, want, secret — always all three.** A quirk is observable in the first minute. A want is what the NPC is trying to get. A secret is what they will not say unprompted. If any of the three is missing, the NPC is a prop.
- **The villain needs a plan.** If the NPC is a villain, give them a goal the party can understand, a plan for what happens if the party does nothing, and a reason they are hard to simply kill.
- **No non-SRD mechanics by default.** If the NPC needs a stat block, use an SRD stat block (commoner, guard, noble, mage, and so on) and reskin it. Say which block you used. Homebrew stats are labelled homebrew.
- **Never play the players' side.** Write what the NPC says and does. Never write what a player character says, thinks, or decides in response.
- **Keep it playable.** The DM reads this aloud or paraphrases it. Short lines, concrete details, no paragraphs of backstory the players will never hear.

## The workflow

1. **Name and first impression.** A name that fits the setting, plus the one thing the party notices first — a voice, a smell, a visible contradiction.
2. **Quirk.** One observable trait: a verbal tic, a habit, a visible contradiction. Something the players can imitate at the table five minutes later.
3. **Want.** What the NPC is trying to get, in one sentence. The want is the hook — it is what makes the party care, help, or oppose them.
4. **Secret.** What they are hiding, and what it would cost them if it came out. The secret is the plot.
5. **Voice.** Two or three example lines in the NPC's voice — a greeting, a refusal, a plea. Write them as dialogue the DM can read aloud.
6. **Hook and consequence.** What the NPC asks of the party, and what happens if the party ignores them, helps them, or betrays them. The world moves without the players.
7. **Deliver.** Output in the format below.

## Output format

```markdown
## <Name> — <role>

**First impression:** <one line — what the party sees and hears first>
**Quirk:** <one line>
**Want:** <one line — what they are trying to get>
**Secret:** <one line — what they are hiding, and what it costs if it comes out>

**Voice:**
- Greeting: "<line>"
- Refusal: "<line>"
- Plea: "<line>"

**Hook:** <what they ask of the party, in one or two sentences>
**If ignored:** <what happens>
**If helped:** <what happens>
**If betrayed:** <what happens>

**Stat block:** <SRD stat block used, e.g. Noble, or "Commoner — no combat stats needed">
```

## Voice bank — pick one, then commit

- **The tired professional:** has seen it all, charges accordingly, sighs before answering.
- **The too-friendly stranger:** warm, curious, asks too many questions, remembers everything.
- **The nervous true believer:** speaks fast, quotes a creed, flinches at doubt.
- **The dry wit:** says the bleak thing plainly, then moves on like it was weather.
- **The one with a debt:** owes someone, everyone knows it, will do almost anything to clear it.

## Fold&Roll voice

Eerie first, funny a beat later. A joke next to something unsettling, horror with dry nervous wit, never graphic. If the NPC is funny, the humour comes from a specific, human detail — not from a joke told at the players. If the NPC is frightening, the fright comes from what they *do not* say.

## When the players latch on

Players will adopt a throwaway NPC and make them central. That is a feature, not a problem. If the user says the party has latched onto an NPC, develop them further: give them a second want, a connection to a faction, and a scene where they ask the party for something that costs them.
