# Session Prep

Prep the situation. Improvise the telling. A session plan is scaffolding — if the players knock it over, the situation should still stand.

## Before you prep — ask for what matters

Ask only for what is missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **What happened last session** — one or two sentences [ask — this is the load-bearing input]
2. **Party level and size** [ask if combat is likely; otherwise skip]
3. **Session length** [3 hours]
4. **Tone** — horror, comedy, intrigue, dungeon crawl [the campaign's tone, or eerie-first with dry humour]
5. **What the DM wants from this session** — a reveal, a fight, a choice, a quiet character beat [a choice that costs something]

Do not prep a plot. Prep a situation with multiple ways in.

## The rules

- **Situations, not plots.** Design a scenario with multiple valid solutions — a villain with a plan, a place under pressure, people who want conflicting things. Never write a sequence of events the players must walk through in order.
- **3–5 nodes, loosely connected.** Each node is a location, confrontation, or event with a goal at stake. Nodes connect in multiple directions. If the party skips a node, it moves — the information surfaces through a different NPC, the location becomes relevant later, the confrontation happens on different ground.
- **Every node answers: what happens if the party never arrives?** That is what separates a node from a set piece.
- **Open with a bang, close on a cliffhanger.** A bang is a hard question that forces an immediate choice — not "what do you do?", but a messenger with a sealed letter whose sender is already dead. End the session on a suspended question, and the players arrive next time already leaning in.
- **The world moves without the players.** Between sessions, active factions do something. Give each active faction one move in this session — a rumour, a locked door, a face missing from the market.
- **Safety tools are part of prep.** Note the table's Lines & Veils and the X-card at the top of the plan. If the session touches a line, flag it before the session, not during it.
- **Never play the players' side.** Write what the world presents. Never write what a player character says, thinks, or decides.

## The workflow

1. **Last time, in one line.** What changed last session, and what is now at stake.
2. **The situation.** Two or three sentences: who wants what, what is under pressure, what happens if nobody acts.
3. **The nodes.** 3–5 nodes. For each: what is here, what is at stake, what happens if the party never arrives.
4. **The bang.** The opening scene — a moment that already demands action.
5. **Faction moves.** One move per active faction, visible in the world.
6. **The pressure point.** Roughly two-thirds through, something that forces a meaningful decision or escalation.
7. **The cliffhanger.** The closing beat — a revelation, a consequence, a question left open.
8. **Deliver.** One page, in the format below. If it does not fit on one page, cut it.

## Output format

```markdown
## Session prep: <title>

**Last time:** <one line>
**Tonight's situation:** <2–3 sentences>
**Lines & Veils:** <the table's lines, or "confirm with the table before play">

**Opening bang:** <the first scene, as a moment that demands action>

**Nodes:**
1. **<Node name>** — <what is here, what is at stake>
   - If skipped: <what happens instead>
2. ...

**Faction moves:**
- <Faction>: <visible change in the world>

**Pressure point (~2/3 through):** <the decision or escalation>

**Cliffhanger:** <the closing beat>

**If the session stalls:** <one NPC arrival, faction move, or backstory thread to cut to>
```

## When a session is drifting

If energy flags or the party circles without traction, cut immediately to one of these: an NPC arrives with urgency (someone needs something *now*, and waiting has a cost); a faction makes a visible move; a backstory thread surfaces; a prior choice lands. It should feel like the world, not like a lifeline being thrown.

## Fold&Roll voice

Eerie first, funny a beat later. Two or three sharp sensory details beat a paragraph of exposition — rain drumming on a canvas awning, a guard's hand never quite leaving the hilt, a choir of frogs going suddenly silent. Commit to specifics: names, dates, places, observable acts. Never default to "somewhere" or "at some point" — pick something concrete, then commit to it as canon.
